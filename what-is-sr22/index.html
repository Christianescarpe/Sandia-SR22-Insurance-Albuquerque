What Is an SR-22?

If you have been told you need an SR-22 in Albuquerque, you may wonder what that means for your insurance and license. First and foremost: an SR-22 is not an insurance policy by itself. It is an official Certificate of Financial Responsibility that your insurance carrier files with the state to verify you carry the minimum liability coverage required by law.

Why It Is Called a Certificate of Financial Responsibility

The SR-22 serves as legal proof that you are actively insured. Once submitted, your insurer is legally required to notify the Motor Vehicle Division if your coverage ever cancels or lapses. Because insurers take on continuous reporting duties for high-risk profiles, policies carrying an SR-22 endorsement require specialized handling.

Under New Mexico MVD auto insurance regulations, all motorists operating on state roads must maintain liability coverage of at least $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 per accident, and $10,000 property damage. Regulations & Rules

Who Typically Needs an SR-22 Certificate

Drivers usually encounter an SR-22 requirement following major driving violations or court orders, such as:

  • A DWI conviction or administrative license revocation
  • Operating a motor vehicle without active liability insurance
  • Accumulating excessive moving violation points within a 12-month span
  • Court-mandated reinstatement conditions or ignition interlock licensing
  • Moving to New Mexico with an outstanding out-of-state SR-22 requirement

If you need step-by-step guidance on clearing your record, our step-by-step Albuquerque filing process page details everything from quote comparison to MVD verification.

How Long You Must Maintain an SR-22

In most situations involving license reinstatement, drivers are mandated to keep an SR-22 active continuously for three years. Maintaining uninterrupted coverage is essential: a lapse of even one day triggers an alert to the MVD, which can reset your requirement timeline and re-suspend your driving privileges.

SR-22 Endorsements vs. Standard Auto Policies

An auto policy with an SR-22 endorsement provides the same core protections as a regular policy — bodily injury and property damage liability, plus optional collision and comprehensive coverage. The primary difference is the state reporting mechanism and the risk tier of the policy. Working with a specialist ensures you find carriers that offer competitive rates for your specific record.

What Happens If Your Coverage Lapses

Allowing an SR-22 policy to lapse due to non-payment or missed renewal results in an immediate notice sent to state authorities. This can lead to vehicle registration suspension, license revocation, and additional reinstatement fees. Common Misconceptions About SR-22s

One common myth is that an SR-22 remains on your record permanently. In truth, once your required period is fulfilled, you can transition back to standard insurance underwriting. Another myth is that you must own a car; non-owner filings protect non-vehicle owners completely. Finally, you do not have to handle the filing paperwork yourself — your licensed insurer handles electronic submission directly.
