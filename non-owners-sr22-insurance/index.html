Understanding Non-Owner Policy Coverage

A non-owner SR-22 policy provides bodily injury and property damage liability protection when you drive a vehicle you do not own — such as a borrowed vehicle, a rental car, or a friend's car. It does not cover physical damage to the vehicle itself, which is why monthly premiums are substantially lower than standard owner insurance. It satisfies state legal requirements while keeping your costs manageable.

Under New Mexico MVD minimum liability requirements, drivers must carry coverage meeting the state baseline of 25/50/10 ($25,000 per person bodily injury, $50,000 per occurrence, and $10,000 property damage). A non-owner policy satisfies this standard completely.

Who Should Consider Non-Owner Coverage

A non-owner SR-22 policy is an ideal solution if you:

  • Need to reinstate your New Mexico driver's license for work or family but do not own a vehicle
  • Use public transportation, rideshare services, or borrow cars occasionally
  • Are between vehicles and want to prevent a lapse in your continuous coverage record
  • Want the most cost-effective way to satisfy a court or MVD mandate

To review baseline qualifications, check our SR-22 eligibility guidelines for a complete explanation.

Why Non-Owner Policies Cost Less

Because non-owner coverage excludes collision and comprehensive physical damage for a designated vehicle, the underwriting risk is lower. You are covered strictly for third-party liability if you are involved in an at-fault accident while operating someone else's vehicle.

Limitations of Non-Owner Insurance

Non-owner policies do not cover vehicles owned by members of your household or vehicles provided for regular commercial use. Furthermore, if you purchase a vehicle during your required filing period, you must notify your insurer immediately to transition to an owner policy.
